The Worst Car Plant in America: What NUMMI Teaches Us About PRINCE2 7's People Element

Website segment: Frameworks In The Enterprise

https://pdcaconsulting.com/nummi-people-element-prince2-7/

General Motors did not have a people problem at Fremont.

That is the uncomfortable part of the NUMMI story.

GM closed the plant as one of the worst in America. Toyota reopened it with mostly the same workforce, the same union, and the same physical site. Within a couple of years, it was producing at a level GM had said was impossible there.

So what changed?

Not the people. The system around the people.

That is exactly why PRINCE2 7 was right to move People into the method as an integrated element. Culture, leadership, communication, collaboration and change are not decoration around project management. They decide whether the method survives contact with the organisation.

If you still treat people as a resourcing line, NUMMI is worth another look.

Don't Pave the Cowpath: What Boston's Streets Teach Us About ITIL (Version 5)'s Last Two Principles

Website segment: Frameworks In The Enterprise

https://pdcaconsulting.com/dont-pave-the-cowpath-itil-version-5/

Every organisation has a Boston hidden somewhere in its process estate.

A lane was added because it made sense at the time. Then another. Then a workaround. Then an exception. Then a report nobody reads. Years later, the organisation calls the result governance.

Then someone points AI or automation at it and declares progress.

That is paving the cowpath.

Boston is a useful warning because no one set out to design a maze. The maze emerged because each small decision was left in place too long.

ITIL (Version 5)'s final two guiding principles matter because they come in the right order: keep it simple and practical, then optimise and automate.

Reverse that order and you have only built a faster mess.

Holiday Reading, 1954 Vintage: Drucker and the Seven ITIL (Version 5) Guiding Principles

Website segment: Frameworks In The Enterprise

https://pdcaconsulting.com/drucker-itil-version-5-guiding-principles/

I took a 1954 management book on holiday. Of course I did.

Peter Drucker's The Practice of Management is not new, not fashionable, and not trying to impress anyone with language.

That is what makes it so useful.

Reading it beside the seven ITIL (Version 5) guiding principles, the striking thing is not how modern ITIL sounds. It is how old the best service management wisdom really is.

Focus on value was in Drucker's Cadillac story. Start where you are was in Sears. Iteration and feedback were in management by objectives. Keep it simple and practical was in his attack on useless reports. Optimise before automating was already clear before most organisations had seen a computer.

The terminology is newer. The discipline is not.

Building the Cathedral: Drucker and the Seven PRINCE2 7 Principles

Website segment: Frameworks In The Enterprise

https://pdcaconsulting.com/drucker-prince2-7-principles/

The third stonecutter is the one every project needs.

In Drucker's famous story, three men are asked what they are doing.

One is earning a living. One is cutting stone. The third is building a cathedral.

That is not a sentimental management tale. It is a governance test.

Do the people on the project understand the product, the purpose, the justification, the roles, the stage they are in, and the tolerances they are trusted to work within?

PRINCE2 7's seven principles are often taught as method rules. I think they are better understood as a way of keeping the cathedral visible while the daily work is still stone cutting.

Without that, projects become busy, tidy and pointless.

The Machine Meets Its Match: Eric Kimberling's Welcome to the Machine and PRINCE2 Agile Version 2

Website segment: Frameworks In The Enterprise

https://pdcaconsulting.com/kimberling-prince2-agile-version-2/

Most enterprise software disasters are sold before they are delivered.

The eighteen month plan nobody believes. The expert team that disappears after the sale. The implementation partner who becomes the only person who understands your own system. The assurance process that quietly serves the vendor more than the buyer.

Eric Kimberling's Welcome to the Machine is useful because it names the machinery.

PRINCE2 Agile Version 2 is useful because it gives buyers a way not to be processed by it.

Stage boundaries create scheduled honesty. Defined roles make accountability visible. Business justification keeps the stopping question alive. The Agilometer asks the awkward questions before the contract becomes a trap. The People element reminds us that capability has to be built inside the organisation, not rented forever.

That is not bureaucracy. It is self defence.
