Prospect Engine — Master Sales Outreach SOP & Workflow

Reusable across every sales project

Last updated 2026-06-02  ·  Prospect Engine LLC  ·  LinkedIn-first B2B outreach, fully personalized, human voice, nothing templated

The one-line formula (same for every client):
Listen → reply in voice → refine on feedback → source & score leads (fit gate, then intent) → warm → noteless connect → M1 to M4 personalized DMs (any reply stops it forever) → triage inbox & reply same day → book the call → keep content one batch ahead → track, report, commit to git. Live-check before every send. Never mix clients. Never publish without client approval.

2.ICP & persona definition

Fit is the gate. A prospect must pass firmographic fit before intent is even checked. If they fail fit, they never enter the list, no matter how strong the signal.

2.1 The fit gate (firmographic)

Define per project: business type (B2B that sells to businesses), primary segments, company size (sweet spot is usually 11–50 employees), stage/funding, geography (the priority markets), and the one-line "why us fits" test. Write the one-line test for the project, e.g.:

One-line test "Does this person own a revenue or growth number, and would what we sell obviously help them in the next 90 days?" If yes → candidate. If no → drop.

2.2 Decision-maker personas (who we message)

Most projects map to four personas. Tune titles + pains + the service to lead with per client.

PersonaTitlesCore painLead with
A — Founder / CEO (primary)Founder, Co-Founder, CEO, MD"I am the bottleneck, pipeline is inconsistent."Revenue + GTM strategy
B — Revenue / Growth leaderHead/VP/Dir Sales, Revenue, Growth, Marketing; CRO; CMO"Reps do admin not selling, reply rates dropping."Revenue + AI automation
C — Agency owner / PartnerFounder/CEO/Partner of agencies"We fill clients' funnels, our own is feast or famine."Done-for-you outbound + Operations
D — Ops / COO (automation entry)COO, Head of Ops, founder wearing ops hat"Manual reporting, spreadsheet chaos, founder is the system."AI automation + Software + Ops

A and C convert best for a founder-led pitch and should be the bulk of the list. B and D widen the funnel.

2.3 Per-market notes & disqualifiers

Record tone per market (e.g. direct/numbers-first; plain/no-hype; precision/proof/GDPR-aware; warm/relationship-led). Hard-stop exclusions (never add): direct competitors, existing clients or anyone already in another PE campaign (dedupe against all client folders), obvious "no budget" signals (student, intern, between roles, solopreneur with no team), and enterprise with a mature in-house SDR org.

3.Research & lead sourcing

We do not "find names." We run pools through a pipeline until we have scored, verified, tiered prospects. Fit is the gate, intent is the ranker, and nothing is random.

3.1 Sources, in priority order

  1. Existing PE / client data first (free and warm). Re-qualify; treat as candidates, not verified (titles drift).
  2. Accelerator cohorts & VC portfolios (named, fresh, ICP-dense). Recent cohorts = founders 0–18 months post-raise = peak intent.
  3. Directories & curated lists (Crunchbase filtered, Tracxn, e27, Wellfound) for breadth + funding/stage.
  4. LinkedIn search on the client's own account — Sales Navigator for precision filters; classic search when SN is unavailable.
  5. Apollo for URLs only. Use it purely to discover ICP people and their LinkedIn URL. Ignore any emails (LinkedIn-only channel).
  6. Third-party intent (G2, hiring scrapes, news) — tie-breaker / ranker only, never a trigger on its own.

3.2 The three mandatory research quality gates

Every prospect must pass all three, in this order, before being shortlisted. Drop after the first failure. No filler rows.

Hard rule Gate 1 — Account is ACTIVE. Posted/commented/reacted in the last 30 days (ideally 14). Check via GET /users/{provider_id}/posts?account_id=[ACCT]&limit=8. 0 posts in 30 days → drop. An inactive account ignores invites and burns the weekly cap.

Gate 2 — Every ICP indication satisfied. Persona match (no near-miss), size in range, industry in scope (reject pure B2C), geography in the priority markets, and commercial autonomy (owns the number or can buy).

Gate 3 — Verified buying intent in the last 60 days. One real, sourced trigger (funding, hiring SDR/AE/CMO/CRO, new launch, new market, leadership change, public GTM-pain post, award). "Verified" means a public URL or LinkedIn post with a date. "Sweet-spot industry" or "no-signal but fits" is filler — drop, do not just rank lower.

25 verified leads beat 50 mixed-quality leads. Never pad a shortlist to hit a number. Never infer a signal ("likely active because the market is hot") — verify or drop.

3.3 Live verification checklist (per row, before it can be contacted)

  • Title + company still match (titles drift — verify live)
  • Headcount still in range
  • Last 3 posts/comments read — capture the live signal + a personal hook
  • Company page checked (recent posts, hiring tab, About for stage/size)
  • Funding / news confirmed with a dated source
  • Persona confirmed (A/B/C/D) + the right service pillar chosen
  • Dedupe against all PE client folders + active campaigns
  • Exclusion sweep (competitor / B2C / enterprise-SDR)
  • Not on any do-not-contact (DNC) list for this client
  • Sending account not on the blocked-account list

3.6 Sourcing rhythm + re-scoring

Run a steady cadence, not one giant scrape. Daily: process one source slice through the fit gate + quick intent pass (~15–25 candidates); live-verify + score yesterday's candidates. Weekly: re-score Tier 1/2 uncontacted (intent decays fast) and the nurture pool, dedupe, and replenish whenever the verified queue drops below one week of outreach volume so the list never runs dry. Re-read intent before every follow-up.

4.Account warm-up & profile optimization

Every invite recipient looks at the profile before accepting, so the profile is the single biggest lever on acceptance, reply rate, and inbound.

4.1 Warm-up-first ramp

On a fresh or low-volume account, do not blast. Ramp invite volume gradually 10 → 20/day over the first 1–2 weeks while the profile and content warm the audience. Bursts trip account-wide invite caps that can lock the account for days (see Section 5).

4.2 Profile build (client applies these on their own profile, in their voice)

  1. Headline — who they help + the operator edge + proof. Keep search keywords (their core service terms). Biggest, fastest lift on acceptance.
  2. About / summary — story + proof (real numbers) + a soft CTA ("send me a message, I will give you an honest read, no pitch").
  3. Featured — pin 3–4 proof items (case-study graphics with the numbers, one strong post, a how-we-work or booking link). Turns a visit into a warm lead.
  4. Banner — positioning in one glance: the line, the proof numbers, a truthful scarcity cue. Big readable text, brand-safe (no third-party logos).
  5. Photo — clear, friendly, senior, eye contact.
  6. Experience — lead each line with an outcome, not a duty.
  7. Skills + recommendations — relevant skills; actively request 5–8 recommendations from past clients. Social proof lifts acceptance.
  8. Creator Mode ON + 5 topics matching the content pillars (unlocks the Top Voice path).
  9. Services / Open-to section listing what the client offers (searchable).
Gotcha Profile edits are the client's to make on their own profile. We draft them and produce the visuals; we do not automate profile changes. Apply the headline + About + Featured before invite volume builds, so invites convert.

5.Connection / invite strategy

Connect requests go out noteless, paced, within the prospect's local working hours, under a weekly cap, on a healthy account, with a real provider_id resolved first.

5.1 Noteless invites — standing rule

Hard rule Send LinkedIn invites without a note. The with-note quota is effectively unusable on these accounts (it is unavailable even manually and trips cannot_resend_yet). Personalize via DM after they accept. The connect-note copy becomes the first post-accept message (M1). (LinkedIn connection-note hard limit is ~200 chars; with-note invites over 200 chars return 400 too_many_characters.)

5.2 Weekly cap, daily throttle, pacing

ControlRule
Weekly cap[WEEKLY_INVITE_CAP] per week, Mon–Sun — NOT a per-day cap. Count week-to-date from the invite log at session start, then send only the remainder spread across the days left.
Daily throttleEffective ceiling is ~25–26 silent invites/day before the account starts returning cannot_resend_yet. Do not burst.
Pacing~5 minutes between successful sends (so 30 invites takes ~2.5 hrs). 30s wait after any failed attempt.
LinkedIn ceiling~80–100 invites/day per account is the absolute platform cap; the weekly quota and ~25/day throttle bite first.
Timezone gateSend only in the prospect's local Mon–Fri 9–18 (shared util Clients\_ops\tz_utils.py), not the sender's.

5.3 ID-format rules — CRITICAL

Hard rule Always resolve the real provider_id first: GET /users/{ACwAA...}?account_id=[ACCT] returns the real provider_id (ACoAA...) and network_distance. Use that for both /users/invite and /chats/messaging. The "swap index 2 w→o" char-swap shortcut is unreliable — the tails differ and it returns 422 invalid_recipient or a misleading 403. Cache the ACw→ACo map to JSON. Only FIRST_DEGREE connections can be DM'd from a non-Premium account.

5.4 Rate-limit handling

  • 429 / disconnected_account → stop hard for the day. Wait 2–3 hours minimum before any retry; refresh cookies if disconnected.
  • cannot_resend_yet on every attempt → this is an account-wide cap, not a per-recipient cooldown. Stop the run immediately. A single 25-invite burst can lock the account 3–4+ days. Do not retry — each failed call wastes the budget and can extend the lock. Resume the following Monday or after the window rolls.
  • already_connected / invalid_recipient → clean skip (already_connected also fires for pending sent invites; use /users/relations as truth).

5.5 Logging

Log every request in the invite log (date, name, id, status). Mark the tracker row connect_sent / invited at the moment of the send, and persist to git before moving to the next contact, so a crash can't lose state and a re-run can't double-send.

6.Nurture & engagement

Warm before you ask, and keep engaging after you connect. This is the single biggest lift to acceptance and reply rates, and it builds the client's authority.

6.1 Pre-touch warming (Tier 1 always)

For 3–5 days before a Tier-1 connect: follow them and their company page, comment on 1–2 recent posts (a sharp add or question, never "great post"), and react to a genuine win. Let the client's name register as a useful peer before the request. The day before a cold message, like or comment on one of their posts.

6.2 Follow the company page before EVERY message

Hard rule Follow the prospect's LinkedIn company page before sending any message, every client, every time (trust-building). Helper: Clients\_ops\follow_company.py (idempotent, never blocks the message if it fails). There is no native Unipile endpoint — resolve the company via GET /linkedin/company/{slug} then follow via the raw voyager feed/dash/followingStates passthrough.

6.3 Daily engagement (client + network posts)

  • Comment on 5–10 in-lane posts/day (prospects + key creators) with a real add (5+ words). Reply to comments on the client's own posts inside the first 60 minutes (the first 60–90 min drive ~70% of reach).
  • Reactions must be a contextual MIX of types (like / insightful / support / celebrate / love), never all "like" — 49 identical likes reads botty. Auto-pick the profile from the post tone.
  • Always like + comment together. A comment without a like reads transactional. Fire the like on the same post after a successful comment.
  • Dedupe before any comment, fail-closed. Live-check the post's comments for our own provider_id first; if we already commented, skip (a double-comment is worse than none). If the comments GET errors (non-retryable), skip the comment to be safe.
  • If the post is outside the client's real lane and there's no honest parallel, like it — do not comment.

6.4 Comment style — the six non-negotiables

  1. No phrase lifting from the post (no quoting their wording back at them).
  2. Identical voice — unmistakably the client/operator, never a generic engagement tool.
  3. Well researched — read the WHOLE post first; if you can't summarize its thesis, you haven't read it.
  4. No repetition — do not restate what they already said.
  5. Cover the WHOLE post — land against the shape of the full argument, not one bullet.
  6. 7th-grade English, human — short sentences, no vendor tone, no em dashes, no emojis.

7.4 The value / soft-ask / break-up logic

One idea per message, one soft ask, never pitch in the first touch. M2 gives value with no ask. M3 makes a low-pressure call ask ("worth a quick 15 minutes?"). M4 closes gracefully and leaves the door open, then the prospect moves to nurture and only re-enters on a fresh trigger. Match the case study to the prospect's industry/region. Use real scarcity truthfully (e.g. "we cap at 10 clients").

7.5 Verify live before EVERY send

Hard rule Before M1/M2/M3/M4, GET /chats/{chat_id}/messages and read the FULL thread oldest→newest. Confirm the expected pre-send state: M1 needs 0 prior messages either way; M2 needs outbound==1, has_reply==False; M3 needs outbound==2, has_reply==False. The newest message must NOT be ours. Any reply, any message from them, anything beyond our own sequence → mark replied, stop forever, no further sends. Never trust the tracker alone; if you can't read the live inbox, skip the send. A "send all" instruction does not override this. Also verify the profile (name, company, title, persona) before each send and adapt or skip on a mismatch.

8.Inbox triage & reply handling

Listen first, every working day. The inbox is the truth and a content/intelligence stream. Reply only to people who belong in our campaign or match the client's ICP.

8.1 Pull & classify

Pull the client's LinkedIn inbox. For every new message log: who, from (origin), what they want, intent class, ICP fit (Y/N + persona + tier), action, status. Classes: prospect reply · warm inbound · networking · vendor pitch · recruiter/HR · client · personal · system · decline.

9.Conversion

The goal of the conversation, not the message, is a short discovery call.

  1. Reply → call CTA. On a positive reply, steer to a short call and offer the booking link [BOOKING_LINK] (example: https://calendly.com/prospect_engine/30min). Frame it as an honest read, not a pitch.
  2. Discovery call. The founder takes every first call (boutique, founder-led). Honest audit framing.
  3. Same-day proposal. A PE differentiator — send the proposal the same day.
  4. Retainer. Close to a retainer. Use real scarcity truthfully ("2 of 10 slots open"), never as a fake tactic.

10.Content & thought leadership

Keep one approved batch ahead, always. Content warms the audience so outreach lands, and pulls inbound.

10.1 The content loop

  1. Draft the next batch in the client's voice: 7th-grade English, no dashes, a strong standalone hook (first 150 chars before "see more"), one idea, one takeaway, a question at the end, a soft CTA. Target 1,250–3,000 chars. Mix 4 TOFU / 2 MOFU / 1 BOFU per week of 7. Always a real number, name where it came from, show a loss not just a win.
  2. Build matching 4K visuals. Vary the visual structure every batch so it never looks templated. Bigger text, brand-safe images only (no third-party logos).
  3. Approval gate. Share the batch as a browser-openable HTML review (never a flat PNG) in the client's WhatsApp group. Refine on their feedback and reconfirm before anything goes live. Never publish without client approval.
  4. Schedule one per day at client local 10:00 via the publisher + Windows task ([PC_FIRE_TIME] on this PC). The publisher is gated: enabled + approved + date == today client-local.
  5. Auto-boost after publish. Reactions from the rotation pool (mixed types, blocked accounts excluded, LRU rotation, no self-react) + one in-voice comment. Keep CTAs/links in the FIRST COMMENT, never the body.
  6. Notify the Operations Team. Immediately share the post link in the "Operations Team" WhatsApp group and ask the team to interact (like, comment, repost). Helper: _ops\notify_ops_post.py. This is a daily, never-skipped step.
  7. Check + reply to comments on the post in voice (concede the point, restate ours softly, no hard CTA to peers; threaded reply; skip own + already-handled).
Gotcha Publishing a feed post is POST /posts (multipart, image under form field attachments); first comment is POST /posts/{id}/comments. To post as a company page add as_organization; to reshare from a profile use repost: <post_id>. Never probe with a real post body (there is no delete for self-posts).

11.Tracking, reporting & delivery

11.1 Trackers (source of truth)

  • Master prospect tracker — one row per prospect, the full schema from 3.5, every touch logged with date + outcome, lifecycle stage from sourced → ... → closed.
  • Invite log — every connection request (used for the week-to-date cap math).
  • Inbox triage log — every inbound message (who, origin, intent, ICP, action, status).
  • CLIENT_TODO.md — open items + a short client personality read, updated as things move.

11.2 Reports

Weekly funnel report vs benchmarks (sourced → invited → connected → M1..M4 → replied → meetings → closed). Monthly pipeline report. CEO reviews before any client-facing summary.

11.3 Delivery flow

Hard rule Deliver client reports as a browser-openable GitHub Pages LINK, never a file or image. (1) Publish the self-contained HTML to ProspectEngineAgentic/reports (Pages, branch main, root) → URL https://prospectengineagentic.github.io/reports/<file>.html; poll until HTTP 200 (deploy takes ~30–90s). (2) Share the link in the "Operations Team" group first so the CEO verifies it. (3) After CEO confirms, share in the client group with a short confident note — no "open in browser", no format talk. Clients prefer links, never attachments.

12.Automation & scheduling

Windows Task Scheduler is the backbone — outreach sends, daily publish, and WhatsApp sync run unattended even with no session open.

12.1 Daily script order per account

Hard rule Invites are priority #1 every day. Run them FIRST on the client's account, before publish/boost/engage/M1, because Unipile shares one rate budget per account — engagement noise can burn the whole week's invite cap before any invite goes out. Order: (1) detect new accepts, (2) invites, (3) M1 to new accepts + due M2–M4, (4) publish + boost reactions/comments (boost uses ROTATION pool accounts, safe to run later or in parallel), (5) inbox + engagement replies last.

12.2 Single-instance rule

Hard rule One sending instance at a time per account. Two senders on one account = LinkedIn 429 rate limit. Run in a visible window, verify with a process check after launch, and check the log before restarting a crashed script (it may have partially completed).

12.3 Task resilience (must be configured)

  • Run on battery + don't stop on battery.
  • Wake the computer to run; Start when available (catch up a missed run).
  • Use the full python.exe path, never bare python.
  • "Run whether the user is logged on or not" requires the account password.
  • Recheck the PC fire time at each DST change (client local 10:00 shifts).

WhatsApp sync runs daily so the store is always fresh. Commit to git after every task — never skip.

Gotcha The penclients repo is NOT connected to the Claude Code GitHub app, so cloud routines with git_repository sources auto-disable. Use the API-fetch pattern (embedded token) for any remote routine. Do not write python -c inline on Windows — write a .py file and run it. A subagent "stop" must kill the loop, not just the child process — rename the drafts file aside and use an explicit stop gate.

13.Voice & copy rules

People reply to people, not to consultants. Write like the client is texting someone they respect.

  • 7th-grade English. Short sentences, simple words, contractions (it's, you're). Read it out loud — if you wouldn't say it to a friend at a cafe, rewrite it.
  • No dashes, no em dashes — ever, in any client-facing copy. British English for UK clients. Match the prospect's language (match the first line of their last post).
  • One real thing per message: a genuine reaction or a plain question, not three polished thoughts.
  • Insight-led — open with a specific observation about THEIR business. Proof by specifics (real numbers), never adjectives. Never fabricate figures.
Banned words / tells outbound, GTM, pipeline, ICP, signal / signal-driven, intent data, leverage, motion, cadence, framework, at scale, activation, value prop, "lines up with", "trade notes", "happy to…", "curious how…", "great connecting", "reaching out to business leaders like yourself", "hope this finds you well", "just checking in", "quick question". No abstract noun stacks. No balanced "X is the easy part, Y is the work" parallels. No emojis, no ALL CAPS, no "!!!".

14.Hard invariants (never break)

  4. Never mix clients — separate folders, separate git, no overlap.
  5. Never publish to a client's live profile without their approval.

  11. Real numbers only — never fabricate proof or claims.
  12. Commit to git after every task.

15.Cadence checklists

15.1 Daily (per active client)

  • Read WORKFLOW.md + clients.json + this client's OPERATING_PLAN.md
  • Sync WhatsApp; read the client group; reply in voice or draft + flag CEO
  • Refine any shared work the client gave feedback on; reconfirm
  • LISTEN: pull the inbox, classify + log every message; stop any sequence that got a reply
  • RESPOND: draft replies for prospect replies first, then warm inbound; route the rest
  • Detect new accepts; send invites FIRST (within weekly cap, prospect-local hours)
  • Send due M1–M4 after a fresh live-thread check; follow the company page first
  • ENGAGE: comment on 5–10 in-lane posts (mix reactions + like), reply to comments on own posts
  • SOURCE: process one slice toward the target; verify + score yesterday's candidates
  • Publish the day's approved post at client local 10:00; boost; notify Ops group; reply to its comments
  • Update master tracker + CLIENT_TODO; commit to git

  • Re-score uncontacted Tier 1/2 + the nurture pool; dedupe
  • Replenish the verified queue if below one week of volume
  • Build + share the next content batch for approval (one batch ahead)

16.New-project quick-start runbook

Day 1

  1. Onboarding (Section 1): folder, CLIENT.md, credentials check + account health, blocklist check, register in clients.json, git folder.
  2. Define ICP + personas + offer + proof points + disqualifiers (Section 2). Set the weekly cap + timezone.
  3. Draft profile optimization (Section 4) and hand to the client to apply.

Week 1

  1. Source + run the first pool through the three quality gates; live-verify + score + tier (Section 3). Aim ~20 verified/day.
  2. Warm-up-first: engage on Tier-1 prospects' content; ramp invites 10 → 20/day, noteless, prospect-local hours, paced (Section 5).
  3. Stand up content: draft the first approved batch (Section 10), set the publisher + Windows publish task at client local 10:00.
  4. Begin daily inbox triage (Section 8) and the daily engagement hour (Section 6).

Steady state

  1. Run the daily checklist (15.1) every working day on the invites-first order (Section 12), committing to git after each task.
  2. M1→M4 sequence on new accepts with a live-thread check before every send (Section 7); any reply → stop → convert (Section 9).
  3. Keep content one batch ahead; weekly re-score + replenish + funnel report; monthly pipeline report via Pages link.
  4. On any error: recover + patch the script + commit. Learn and improve the next batch.
