Day 1Training

A certificate will not fix a broken team

A certificate will not fix a broken team. I say this as someone who has trained thousands of people. Training matters. But on its own it is not enough. A course gives your team a shared language and a set of tools. What decides if any of it sticks is the place they go back to on Monday. Here is what I see in teams where training actually lands: The manager joins the conversation, not just the budget line. People get a little time to try the new way before the old habits pull them back. One or two changes are picked on purpose, instead of trying to boil the ocean. Small wins get noticed out loud. When those things are missing, even the best course fades in a few weeks. The slides were never the problem. So before you book any training, ask one quiet question. What will be different in the room when they come back. That answer matters more than the course itself. Thinking about training that actually sticks for your team? Day 2PRINCE2

PRINCE2 is not red tape

PRINCE2 is not red tape. This is the myth I hear most. People picture binders, forms, and meetings about meetings. That is not PRINCE2. That is PRINCE2 used badly. One of its core ideas is tailoring. You are meant to fit the method to the size and risk of the project, not drown a small job in heavy process. A two week piece of work and a two year programme should not carry the same paperwork. PRINCE2 actually tells you to scale it. Here is the simple test I give teams: Every document should help someone make a decision. If a report has no reader and no decision, drop it. Keep what protects the project, lose what only protects someone's back. Done well, PRINCE2 is light, clear, and calm. It tells everyone who decides what, and what good looks like. The paperwork was never the point. The control was. Where has process slowed your projects down lately? Want PRINCE2 tailored to how your team really works? Day 3Service value

Closing tickets fast is not good service

Closing tickets fast is not the same as good service. It feels like progress. The queue goes down, the numbers look healthy, everyone relaxes. Then the same problem comes back next week, wearing a different ticket number. Speed is easy to measure, so teams chase it. Value is harder to measure, so it gets ignored. That is the trap. The strongest service teams I work with watch a different set of signals: How often the same issue returns. How many people one fix quietly helps. Whether the business goal behind the service is moving at all. One client was proud of clearing six hundred requests a day. When we looked closer, a handful of root causes were creating most of them. Fix those, and the queue shrinks on its own. So this week, look past the count. Ask what your tickets are trying to tell you. The pattern is usually louder than the pile. Day 4Problem management

Two million euros, no new tools

One client saved two million euros a year. They did not buy a single new tool. Here is what actually happened. They had the same handful of system errors firing over and over. Each one looked small. Each one had a quick workaround. So nobody ever stopped to fix the cause. We used one simple ITIL habit. Problem management. We listed the errors that came back most. We found the few real causes behind them. We fixed those, not the symptoms. The repeat incidents dropped. The teams stopped firefighting. And the cost of all that wasted effort, counted across a full year, came to about two million euros. No new platform. No big project. Just the discipline to ask why this keeps happening, and the time to answer it properly. Most teams already have this saving sitting in their incident data. They are just too busy clearing tickets to go and find it. What is the one error your team has quietly worked around for months? Curious what this could look like for you? Day 5ITIL & AI

Your AI assistant still thinks it is ITIL 4

Your AI assistant still thinks it is ITIL 4. Ask one to explain the new ITIL and watch. Many will give you ITIL 4 content, the old name, and a confident smile. It is not being lazy. It learned from years of older writing, and the new ITIL is still fresh. The internet has not caught up yet, so neither has the model. This is a useful reminder for anyone leaning on AI for ITIL or PRINCE2 work. It is brilliant for a first draft. It is risky as a final answer. It cannot tell you what changed last month if it never read it. The same rule we use for people applies to tools. Check the source. Know who wrote it and when. A good trainer reads the new material the day it lands. A model waits for the world to write about it first. That gap is where the mistakes live. So use AI to go faster. Just keep a human who has actually read the current guidance between it and your team. Have you caught your AI getting the new ITIL wrong yet?
