For your review

What this is, and what we are going to do

Robert, this is your back catalogue of people you once had a real conversation with that then went quiet. Not fresh cold contacts. They showed interest, offered a referral, floated a partnership, or had a meeting that lapsed, and the thread has been silent since roughly last November.

The plan is simple. We are going to warm these relationships back up with a short, human hello written in your voice. Each one acknowledges the time gap, shares one soft update (your new ITIL Master across all six modules at trainer level, and that you are busy delivering courses), and asks one light question about how they are doing now. This is staying in touch, not a sales pitch.

Many of these people are training companies, peers, or partners who once wanted you as a trainer or partner, not end clients. So every message is tailored to the relationship, never the same line twice. Below are the first 50, oldest conversation first, for you to read before we begin.

How we will send them

  • Starting tomorrow, oldest conversation first (these run from 531 down to 259 days quiet).
  • We check the live chat before every single message. If you have already replied, or they have, we skip them and never message again.
  • Any reply comes straight to you. You handle the warm conversations yourself, as always.
  • Each message is tailored to whether they are a partner, a peer, or a possible client (the colour tags below).
  • Where someone changed jobs, we drop the old company and write for their new role.
  • Same day, we also like and leave one short comment on their recent post from your profile, to warm them up first.
  • Gentle pace: about 12 a day, five minutes apart, only in their local working hours. The full batch goes out over roughly four working days.
  • Your voice throughout: British English, plain and warm, no dashes, one light question, signed off as you.

If you want any of these reworded, or you would rather we hold any single person, just tell us and we will adjust before that one goes out.
