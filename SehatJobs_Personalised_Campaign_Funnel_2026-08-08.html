Executive Campaign Design

Result We Need

Qualified conversations with Malaysian hospital decision makers, handled directly from Santhya's LinkedIn profile.

Primary meeting target:

  • HR or Talent Acquisition decision maker.
  • Director of Nursing or clinical nursing leader.
  • Operations, COO, CEO, MD, or group leader who can sponsor and route internally.

Do not chase small clinics unless the company has a clear hospital-level or specialist-centre need. Santhya said most clinic-level hiring is too small or not qualified enough for Sehat's strongest offer.

Approach Order

  1. HR and Talent Acquisition.
  2. Nursing or Clinical leadership.
  3. Operations or COO.
  4. CEO, MD, Group CEO, Founder, Board level.

Why:

  • HR controls access and can block the process.
  • Nursing feels the staffing pain most directly.
  • Operations feels capacity, ward, service, and expansion risk.
  • CEO or MD usually delegates back to HR or operations, so the goal is routing or sponsorship.

Non-Negotiable Campaign Rules

  • Sender is Santhya's LinkedIn ID only.
  • All connection notes and post-connect messages must sound like Santhya, not Prospect Engine.
  • No automated DM after connect.
  • No deck in first message.
  • No price in first message.
  • No "we are an agency" framing.
  • No "we can solve your shortage" claim.
  • No university outreach until Santhya gives the separate university offer.
  • Do not mention Nurses.mine in outreach until Santhya approves.
  • Do not use WhatsApp unless the number is clearly public or business-facing and LinkedIn or email has already been tried.
  • DNC and relationship-only accounts are blocked unless Santhya explicitly clears them.

Sehat Jobs Offer, In Plain Business Language

What Santhya Is Really Selling

Sehat Jobs is not just selling candidate sourcing.

It is selling a healthier nurse workforce pipeline for Malaysian healthcare employers that struggle with:

  • repeated nursing vacancies,
  • specialist nurse gaps,
  • expansion or new ward readiness,
  • recruitment drives,
  • candidate no-shows,
  • salary mismatch,
  • nurse retention after hiring,
  • HR teams being stretched.

Why Hospitals Resist

Santhya said hospitals often do not want agencies because:

  • agency fees feel expensive,
  • HR does not want to look replaced,
  • nurses may leave after a fee is paid,
  • hospitals protect nurses strongly because everyone needs them,
  • big hospitals have their own HR process.

So our language must position Sehat as:

  • HR support,
  • workforce pipeline support,
  • nurse audience access,
  • specialist nurse access,
  • recruitment-drive support,
  • not an HR replacement.

Sehat's Proof Points

Use these only when relevant and never all at once:

  • Healthcare and nurse recruitment experience at management level, around 30 years.
  • About 2,000 post-basic specialist nurses in Sehat's database.
  • A large nurse community, described by Santhya as about 30,000.
  • A growing candidate database, described in the call as 15,000 to 20,000 plus.
  • Strong nurse-side audience, including TikTok/social reach.
  • Google reviews and prior hospital/university work.
  • Candidate preparation before joining.
  • Post-placement contact so Sehat does not disappear after placement.

Core Offer Models

  1. Flexible recruitment support.
  • 1 month to 6 month guarantee options.
  • Useful when HR wants cost and retention flexibility.
  1. Nurses on Demand.
  • A portal/menu of nurse profiles.
  • Hospitals can review specialty, qualification, and salary expectations.
  • Strongest proof that Sehat is not old-school.
  • Best used after the prospect shows interest, not in the connection note.
  1. Volume hiring model.
  • For 11 or more roles per year.
  • Up to 20 percent discount.
  • Dedicated recruiter.
  • Best for hospital groups, expansions, new branches, new wards, recruitment drives.
  1. End-to-end or modular support.
  • Source, shortlist, organise recruitment events, support interview flow, documentation, onboarding, and orientation.
  • If the hospital has strong HR, Sehat can support only the part they need.
  1. Non-recruitment routes.
  • Hiring events.
  • Broadcast to Sehat's nurse community.
  • Employer branding or social review.
  • Use when HR rejects agency placement but still wants nurse audience access.

Market Context To Use Carefully

Do not overload prospects with stats. Use one market sentence only if needed.

Safe wording:
Malaysia's nurse shortage is now a public workforce issue, so many hospitals are reviewing how to keep their nurse pipeline healthier.

Validated external context:

  • RTM reported on 4 April 2026 that KKM targeted about 15,000 public nursing vacancies to fill in 2026.
  • CNA reported Malaysia's health minister saying the nurse shortage could reach close to 60 percent by 2030.
  • The Star reported Malaysia had 120,667 registered nurses last year and about 14,000 public healthcare nurse shortages.

Do not write to prospects:

  • "You are short of nurses."
  • "Malaysia has a 60 percent shortage, so you need us."
  • "We can fill all your vacancies."

HeyReach Campaign Architecture

Sender setup:

  • Use Santhya's LinkedIn ID as the only sender profile.
  • Prospect Engine prepares, reviews, and operates the campaign, but the prospect-facing voice is Santhya.
  • Do not run this from Saif, Prospect Engine, or any other internal LinkedIn profile.
  • Before launch, confirm Santhya's LinkedIn account is connected, healthy, and selected correctly inside HeyReach.

Pre-Connect Sequence

Use this sequence in HeyReach from Santhya's LinkedIn profile:

  1. Visit profile.
  2. If there is a relevant recent post, like one post.
  3. Wait 2 to 4 days.
  4. Send persona-specific connection note.
  5. If no acceptance after 7 to 10 days, follow profile.
  6. If still no acceptance after 14 to 21 days, move to manual review for email or strategic hold.

Do not endorse before connection. Endorsement can look fake if there is no relationship.

After Connection

Stop automation.

Manual workflow:

  1. Read prospect profile.
  2. Read company page and website.
  3. Check DNC and Santhya relationship status.
  4. Identify one likely pressure.
  5. Pick one offer angle.
  6. Write a custom M1.
  7. Send only after sanity check.

Persona Lane 1: HR and Talent Acquisition

Role Examples

HR Director, CHRO, Head of HR, Head of People, Talent Acquisition Lead, Recruitment Manager, Human Capital Manager, HR Manager.

What HR Cares About

  • Filling roles without losing process control.
  • Reducing low-quality applicant flow.
  • Avoiding expensive agency mistakes.
  • Candidate no-shows.
  • Nurses leaving after fees.
  • Keeping internal HR credible.

Approach Style

Respect HR. Do not provoke agency resistance early.

Best angle:
"Support your nurse pipeline without taking control away from HR."

HeyReach Connection Notes

Use A by default. Use B when the title is senior.

A. HR standard:
Hi {{first_name}}, I saw your HR work with {{company}}. I focus on Malaysia healthcare hiring and nurse workforce topics. Thought it would be good to connect.

B. Senior HR:
Hi {{first_name}}, I noticed you lead people work at {{company}}. I share practical notes on healthcare hiring and nurse workforce planning in Malaysia. Thought it would be good to connect.

C. Recruitment/Talent:
Hi {{first_name}}, I saw your talent role with {{company}}. I work around nurse hiring pipelines for Malaysian healthcare employers. Thought it would be good to connect.

Manual M1 Formula

Structure:

  1. One profile/company observation.
  2. Respect HR ownership.
  3. Mention one likely pressure.
  4. Ask one diagnostic question.

Template:
Hi {{first_name}}, thanks for connecting.

I noticed {{company_context}}. For a hospital team, nurse hiring usually has to stay close to HR because candidate quality, no-shows, and retention all affect the process.

My team at Sehat Jobs supports HR teams with nurse pipeline options, from flexible hiring support to profile-led review through Nurses on Demand.

Is nursing hiring something your team is actively reviewing this quarter?

HR Conversation Path

If they reply with interest:
Ask:
Is the current need more one-off hiring, repeated vacancies, or a larger recruitment drive?

If one-off:
I can keep the model light and flexible, depending on which guarantee option fits.

If repeated vacancies:
Sehat can help HR keep an active nurse pipeline instead of restarting search each time.

If bulk hiring:
If the need is 11 or more roles across the year, I can explain the volume model and dedicated recruiter option.

Meeting CTA:
Would it be useful to compare the hiring model for 15 minutes and see if this is relevant for {{company}}?

HR Objection Handling

"We do not work with agencies."
Reply:
I understand. Many HR teams feel the same. The reason I reached out is that Sehat can also support in lighter ways, such as nurse audience access, hiring events, or a profile menu before any placement discussion.

Question:
Would nurse audience access be more relevant than placement support right now?

"Agency fees are expensive."
Reply:
Fair point. I built a few models because not every hospital wants the same fee or guarantee structure.

Question:
Is your usual challenge cost, candidate quality, or retention after joining?

"Nurses leave after we pay."
Reply:
That is exactly the issue we try to address. Sehat prepares candidates before joining and stays close after placement, so the work does not stop at the fee.

Question:
Is retention after joining currently a bigger concern than sourcing?

Persona Lane 2: Nursing and Clinical Leadership

Role Examples

Director of Nursing, Chief Nursing Officer, Head of Nursing, Matron, Nursing Manager, Clinical Director, Head of Clinical Services, Ward or specialist unit leader.

What Nursing Cares About

  • Enough qualified nurses to run wards.
  • Specialist nurses for ICU, paediatrics, emergency, critical care, OT, dialysis, perioperative, and similar units.
  • HR process moving fast enough.
  • Patient care and staff pressure.

Approach Style

Speak to clinical pain, not agency model.

Best angle:
"You know the real nurse gaps before anyone else does."

HeyReach Connection Notes

A. Nursing standard:
Hi {{first_name}}, I saw your nursing leadership work with {{company}}. I follow Malaysia nurse workforce and specialist staffing topics. Thought it would be good to connect.

B. Clinical services:
Hi {{first_name}}, I noticed your clinical role at {{company}}. I focus on healthcare workforce topics, especially qualified nurse pipelines in Malaysia. Thought it would be good to connect.

C. Specialist unit:
Hi {{first_name}}, I saw your work around {{unit_or_specialty}} at {{company}}. I follow specialist nurse workforce topics in Malaysia. Thought it would be good to connect.

Manual M1 Formula

Template:
Hi {{first_name}}, thanks for connecting.

I noticed your work in {{role_context}} at {{company}}. Nursing leaders usually see the staffing gap first, especially when wards or specialist units need people before HR can move fast enough.

Sehat Jobs has access to post-basic nurse profiles and can help hospitals review relevant nurse options faster, while still keeping HR involved.

Which nursing roles are usually hardest for {{company}} to fill?

Nursing Conversation Path

If they name a role:
That helps. I can check whether Sehat has relevant profiles or a sourcing route for that specialty.

Ask:
Would HR be the right team to include if there is a fit?

If they say HR handles everything:
Yes, HR should stay involved. I wanted to understand the clinical need first so I do not bring a generic recruitment pitch.

Meeting CTA:
Would a short call help to map the hard-to-fill roles and the right HR owner?

Persona Lane 3: Operations and COO

Role Examples

COO, Head of Operations, Hospital Director, Operations Director, General Manager, Head of Business Operations, Centre Manager, Branch Director.

What Operations Cares About

  • Ward readiness.
  • Service line readiness.
  • New branch or new department staffing.
  • Patient flow and capacity.
  • Staffing delays creating revenue or service risk.

Approach Style

Speak in operational terms. Do not start with candidate sourcing.

Best angle:
"Nurse staffing becomes an operations bottleneck when services cannot run at capacity."

HeyReach Connection Notes

A. Operations:
Hi {{first_name}}, I saw your operations role with {{company}}. I follow Malaysia healthcare workforce topics, especially nurse staffing and service readiness. Thought it would be good to connect.

B. COO:
Hi {{first_name}}, I noticed your COO role at {{company}}. I work around healthcare workforce readiness and nurse pipeline topics in Malaysia. Thought it would be good to connect.

C. Hospital Director/GM:
Hi {{first_name}}, I saw your leadership role at {{company}}. I focus on hospital workforce readiness and nurse staffing topics in Malaysia. Thought it would be good to connect.

Manual M1 Formula

Template:
Hi {{first_name}}, thanks for connecting.

I noticed {{company_context}}. From an operations view, nurse hiring is not only an HR issue when it affects ward coverage, service readiness, or expansion timing.

My team at Sehat Jobs supports hospitals with flexible nurse pipeline models, especially when the need is recurring or tied to larger hiring plans.

Is nurse staffing a current bottleneck for any service line or location at {{company}}?

Operations Conversation Path

If they say HR handles hiring:
That makes sense. HR should stay central. I asked from the operations side because capacity pressure often starts with your team before it becomes a hiring brief.

If they mention expansion/new ward:
That is where I may be useful. I can help decide if this is a one-off hire, a recruitment drive, or a longer nurse pipeline need.

Meeting CTA:
Would it be useful to map the staffing pressure and decide who from HR should be involved?

Manual Personalisation System

Company Signal Tags

Use one primary signal per prospect:

  • Group scale.
  • New branch or expansion.
  • Specialist hospital or specialist centre.
  • Known nursing vacancies.
  • Recruitment drive.
  • High patient volume.
  • Multi-location operations.
  • New service line.
  • No public signal.

Offer Angle Tags

Pick one:

  • HR pipeline support.
  • Specialist nurse access.
  • Nurses on Demand profile review.
  • Volume hiring support.
  • Recruitment drive support.
  • Employer branding or nurse community access.
  • Routing conversation to the right internal owner.

Manual M1 Quality Gate

Before sending any M1, answer:

  1. Did I identify the correct persona lane?
  2. Did I mention one specific company or role context?
  3. Did I avoid sounding like an agency?
  4. Did I ask only one question?
  5. Did I avoid deck, price, and hard sell?
  6. Did I check DNC and Santhya relationship status?

If any answer is no, rewrite.

Channel Escalation Rules

LinkedIn:

  • Primary channel.
  • Connection note only before acceptance.
  • Manual M1 after acceptance.

Email:

  • Use for strategic non-connects only.
  • Best for HR directors, CEOs, and Tier 1 targets where LinkedIn does not accept.
  • Mention LinkedIn attempt only if true.

WhatsApp:

  • Last channel.
  • Use only if the number is public or business-facing.
  • Message must be very short and respectful.
  • If asked how the number was found, answer plainly: public hospital or professional directory.
  • If they object, stop immediately.

WhatsApp sample for approved use:
Hi {{first_name}}, this is Santhya from Sehat Jobs. I tried reaching you on LinkedIn and wanted to ask a quick business question about nurse hiring at {{company}}. If WhatsApp is not the right place, I understand and will not follow up here.
